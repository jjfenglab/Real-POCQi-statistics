You will be shown a clinical question followed by two candidate answers (Answer A and Answer B).

"{note}"

Your job is to summarize the *differences* between Answer A and Answer B as a list of comparative keyphrases, which will be used by a bag-of-words model to find characteristics that lead to an expert clinician rater preferring a particular answer. The keyphrases must be **general enough to apply across unrelated clinical domains** — a phrase extracted from a dermatology question should still be meaningful for a cardiology, nephrology, oncology, neurology, or endocrinology question. If the phrase could only make sense inside one specialty, it is too specific. Hyper-specific phrases are useless to the model because they will not recur across domains.

A keyphrase must be of the form "______: A > B" or "______: B > A".

The blank must be a **general property of a clinical answer**, not a specific clinical entity. To help you brainstorm, here are some axes of variation to consider for characterizing what factors influence clinician rating:

**Content**
- broader scope vs. narrower scope
- comprehensive vs. succinct
- on-topic focus vs. tangential expansion
- population-specific vs. generic
- meandering vs. direct

**Evidence and sourcing**
- cites primary literature vs. unsourced claims
- quantitative evidence vs. qualitative evidence
- references guidelines vs. no guidelines
- cites recommendation strength vs. no evidence grading
- mechanistic reasoning vs. descriptive only
- acknowledges limitations vs. one-sided

**Clinical reasoning and structure**
- broad differential vs. narrow differential
- ranked differential vs. unranked differential
- case-contingent reasoning vs. generic reasoning
- states contraindications vs. omits contraindications
- quantitative thresholds vs. qualitative thresholds
- risk-stratified by subgroup vs. undifferentiated risk
- step-by-step reasoning vs no step-by-step reasoning

**Recommendations and voice**
- definitive recommendation vs. equivocal recommendation
- clinician audience vs. patient audience
- subspecialty-level vs. generalist-level
- actionable guidance vs. conceptual guidance

**Safety**
- more risk-aware vs. less risk-aware
- more adverse effects vs. fewer adverse effects
- more drug interactions vs. fewer drug interactions

**Structure and surface form**
- longer response vs. shorter response
- itemized lists vs. prose paragraphs
- tabulated data vs. no tables

Guidelines:
- **The phrase before the colon must be 2-3 words.** Hard cap at 3 words.
- **The phrase must not name any specific clinical entity** — no disease, drug, organ, body part, lesion feature, test, procedure, or named guideline (e.g., no "biopsy", "ECMO", "NCCN", "lesion", "corticosteroid", "staging", "lymphadenopathy", "pharmacokinetics"). If a difference is tied to a specific entity, abstract it up to the general category — or drop it if no general category fits.
- Examples of abstracting up (BAD → GOOD):
    - "more detailed staging: B > A" → "more diagnostic detail: B > A"
    - "more ECMO pharmacokinetics: B > A" → "more pharmacologic detail: B > A"
    - "more emphasis on lesion morphology: A > B" → "more exam detail: A > B" (or drop)
    - "more emphasis on lesion color: A > B" → drop (too specific; no good abstraction)
    - "includes NCCN guidelines: B > A" → "more named guidelines: B > A"
    - "more biopsy mention: A > B" → "more diagnostic workup: A > B"
    - "cites King 2018: A > B" → "more named studies: A > B"
    - "mentions 97th percentile: B > A" → "more numeric thresholds: B > A"
- For each keyphrase, also list some synonyms and generalizations. **Synonyms must be alternate wordings of the same general concept, not a list of specific sub-aspects.**
    - GOOD synonym bundle (same concept, different wordings): "more diagnostic detail: A > B, more workup detail: A > B, more investigation detail: A > B"
    - BAD synonym bundle (sibling specifics masquerading as synonyms): "more emphasis on lesion color: A > B, more emphasis on lesion texture: A > B, more emphasis on lesion morphology: A > B"
- Synonyms also include switching the comparison sign between A > B and B > A, e.g., "more clinical details: A > B" and "fewer clinical details: B > A"
- Keyphrases, synonyms, and generalizations can be comma-separated or listed as separate strings
- If a property is present in both answers to a similar degree, do not include it (it is not a difference).
- **Always include length as one axis when it differs**, but do not let it dominate: if one answer is longer, still identify at least 5–10 non-length axes that distinguish them. Describe *what fills the extra space* (more caveats? more numbers? more differential? more lifestyle context?) in addition to noting length.
- **Cross-domain test:** before finalizing each keyphrase, ask yourself whether the same phrase would be interpretable and potentially predictive if the question were about an unrelated specialty (cardiology, neurology, nephrology, oncology, endocrinology, dermatology, OB/GYN, etc.). If the phrase relies on domain-specific vocabulary, abstract it up or drop it.
- Only focus on properties of the single-round QA, ignore follow-up offers.
- Return up to 30 entries.
