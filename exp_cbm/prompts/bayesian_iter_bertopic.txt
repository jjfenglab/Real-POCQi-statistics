We are fitting a concept bottleneck model (CBM) that predicts which of two candidate answers to a clinical question an expert physician rater prefers. The outcome Y has two classes: Answer A wins (Y=0) or Answer B wins (Y=1). We want {num_concepts} meta-concepts to extract from each (question, Answer A, Answer B) triple to maximize the accuracy of the CBM.

Each meta-concept is a *comparative* yes/no question of the form "Does Answer B ___ more than Answer A?" — for example, "Does Answer B cite more recent evidence than Answer A?" or "Does Answer B give more specific recommendations than Answer A?". Each concept will later be scored on {-1, 0, 1}: 1 means the property is clearly more true of B than A, -1 means clearly more true of A than B, and 0 means equally true of both or neither.

Interpret the residual model as follows: a cluster with a large positive coefficient means that when at least one of its member keyphrases is extracted for a triple, Answer B is more likely to be preferred than the existing CBM would predict; a large negative coefficient means the opposite (Answer A more likely). Use these residual signals to propose candidate meta-concepts for position {num_concepts}.

Be systematic. Walk down the list from most to least predictive cluster. For each cluster, decide whether it duplicates an existing meta-concept or should become a new candidate. State each candidate as a comparative yes/no question of the form "Does Answer B ___ more than Answer A?".

Rules for good concepts:
- Comparative between A and B (not a single-answer property).
- Objective enough that two reviewers would answer similarly.
- Avoid concepts whose answer is almost always "equally true of both" or almost always one-sided.
- Do not propose unions of two concepts ("Does B cite more recent guidelines or give more specific dosing than A?" is not allowed).
- Do not propose concepts you would expect to agree >95% of the time with any of the existing {num_concepts_fixed} meta-concepts.
- When listing matching words, use example keyphrases drawn from the clusters above.
