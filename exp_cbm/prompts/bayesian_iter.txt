We are fitting a concept bottleneck model (CBM) that predicts which of two candidate answers to a clinical question an expert physician rater prefers. The outcome Y has two classes: Answer A wins (Y=0) or Answer B wins (Y=1). We want {num_concepts} meta-concepts to extract from each (question, Answer A, Answer B) triple to maximize the accuracy of the CBM.

Each meta-concept is a *comparative* yes/no question of the form "Does Answer A ___ more than Answer B?" — for example, "Does Answer A cite more recent evidence than Answer B?" or "Does Answer A give more specific recommendations than Answer B?". Each concept will later be scored on {-1, 0, 1}: 1 means the property is clearly more true of A than B, -1 means clearly more true of B than A, and 0 means equally true of both or neither.

To come up with the remaining meta-concept(s), I first fit a CBM on these {num_concepts_fixed} existing meta-concepts. Then, to figure out how to improve the CBM, I asked an LLM to extract a list of concepts from each (question, A, B) triple, and fit a linear regression on those extracted concepts to predict the residuals of the {num_concepts_fixed}-concept CBM. Interpret the residual model as follows: an extracted concept with a large positive coefficient means that when the concept is more true of Answer A than Answer B, Answer A is more likely to be preferred (above what the existing CBM already predicts); a large negative coefficient means the opposite. Use these residual signals to propose candidate meta-concepts for position {num_concepts}.

Be systematic. Walk down the list from most to least predictive concept. For each entry, decide whether it duplicates an existing meta-concept or should become a new candidate. State each candidate as a comparative yes/no question of the form "Does Answer A ___ more than Answer B?".

Rules for good concepts:
- Comparative between A and B (not a single-answer property).
- Objective enough that two reviewers would answer similarly.
- Avoid concepts whose answer is almost always "equally true of both" or almost always one-sided.
- Do not propose unions of two concepts ("Does A cite more recent guidelines or give more specific dosing than B?" is not allowed).
- Do not propose concepts you would expect to agree >95% of the time with any of the existing {num_concepts_fixed} meta-concepts.
- When listing matching words, just use the exact feature name that shows up in the dataframe, do not include the coefficient.
